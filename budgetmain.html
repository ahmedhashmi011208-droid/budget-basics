Module 01

Budgeting Basics.

A budget is simply a plan for how money can be allocated between requirements, wants and savings.

Income

Money received from allowances, scholarships, internships, part-time work or other sources.

Fixed Expenses

Costs that generally remain similar each month, such as regular transport or fees.

Variable Expenses

Spending that can change from month to month, such as food, shopping or entertainment.

Requirements

Things that are important for daily life, education, health, transportation and similar needs.

Wants

Optional purchases that can often be delayed when money is needed elsewhere.

Savings

Money intentionally kept aside for future goals, emergencies or planned purchases.

Module 02

Needs vs Wants.

Learning to distinguish essential spending from optional spending.

Needs

Items or services important for everyday living, education or essential responsibilities.

Wants

Optional purchases that improve comfort but can often be delayed.

Module 06

Debt & Credit Basics.

Understand how credit cards, buy-now-pay-later and interest work — so you can borrow responsibly and avoid common pitfalls.

01

Credit Cards

A credit card lets you spend money now and pay it back later. If you don't pay the full balance, interest is charged on the remainder.

02

Buy-Now-Pay-Later

BNPL splits a purchase into installments. It can feel free, but missed payments often trigger fees, penalties and credit-impacting defaults.

03

Interest Rates

Interest is the cost of borrowing. A higher rate means you repay more over time — even if the original amount stays the same.

04

Minimum Payments

Paying only the minimum keeps you in debt for longer and costs more overall. Aim to pay the full balance when possible.

05

Credit Score

Your credit score reflects how reliably you repay. On-time payments build it; late or missed payments damage it for years.

06

Debt Traps

Borrowing to repay other debt or relying on credit for essentials is a warning sign. Pause, plan, and seek help if this becomes a pattern.

⚠️ A Simple Rule of Thumb

Educational guidance — not personal financial advice.

  • Pay the full credit card balance every month whenever possible.
  • Only use BNPL for items you already planned to buy — never for impulse purchases.
  • Borrowing to cover needs is a red flag — revisit your budget instead.
  • Total debt repayments should stay well below 20% of your income.
